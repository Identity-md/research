# 1. Question restated

Is Idea D, Open Lane, worth building and launching within 14 days on IMD or CLAUS, and, if so, which of three distinct ideas is the smallest credible proof?

# 2. Verdict in five lines

Gap: **Partly served; yes to a gated IMD pilot**. V4 fee defenses and fee-funded rewards exist, but an LP-approved, agent-authored range plan with payment only after onchain execution is not established by the sources below (Inference, 2026-10-23).
Speed to MVP: **14 days, conditional** on a factory fork proving that its requester fee can be collected by the hook and split in both pool currencies (Inference, 2026-10-23).
MVP: **IMD LP Desk** on Robinhood Chain: one fixed-supply token, one v4 hook, a pro-rata opening batch, one managed LP position, one signed agent plan, and a fee-funded onchain agent payment (Inference, 2026-10-23).
Biggest reason it could fail: no LP other than the sponsor may value qualitative range advice enough to accept the plan, while the factory's existing 1.25% trading fee already competes with cheaper pools (Inference, 2026-10-23; [IMD launch rules](https://imd.fun/docs/)).
Total IMD cost: **1.5 IMD = three orders × 0.5 IMD**, after this research order; gas, liquidity, and agent operating cost are additional and unpriced (Record for unit price, Inference for proposed order count, 2026-10-23; [live capabilities](https://api.imd.fun/requests/capabilities)).

# 3. Competitor table

**Ranked proposals.** **1 — LP Desk (winner):** an AI agent reads unstructured project releases, governance discussions, and competing venue conditions, writes a sourced LP range and expiry, signs it, and executes the accepted plan through a managed vault. The v4 hook recognizes only that signed plan for the managed position; after execution and fee receipt it pays the agent onchain and sends the remaining requester fees to the LP vault. A timed pro-rata opening batch removes transaction-order advantage. No token-level controls, owner-free hook, 24-hour timelock for the order payer, and no ZK. Trading fees never order recurring IMD jobs, the opening rule is deterministic rather than agent-priced, and the pool is on IMD rather than CLAUS; those are the boundaries from Fee to Jobs, Agent First Opening, and Claus Crew. This is the only proposal here whose work quality is purchased directly by the LP rather than inferred from an oracle (Inference, 2026-10-23).

**2 — Catalyst Reserve (short version):** agents read unstructured official disclosures and contract changes, then submit a signed yes/no claim onchain before a panel is ordered. A sample pinned question is: “Did the issuer publish an official notice at the specified URL, before the specified closing block, announcing a pause of the named contract?” The v4 hook uses `afterInitialize`, `beforeSwap`, and `afterSwap` to run the same opening batch, record fee-bearing trades, and direct a fixed portion of the factory's existing requester share to LPs or a reserve according to a fresh [IMD EIP-712 panel attestation](https://imd.fun/docs/). A matching claimant receives a fixed portion onchain only after `questionHash`, chain, expiry, signer, and quorum checks **and actual requester-fee receipt**; a fixed share still goes to LPs. The token is ordinary fixed supply, the hook has no owner setters, payer rights are behind a 24-hour timelock, bundles in the opening get the same pro-rata price, and no ZK is used because it would not remove the panel signer. A sponsor would pay for the first panel and opening liquidity; the smallest test is one resolved claim, one independent LP, and one nonzero fee receipt within 14 days. Its proposed orders are payer deployment, agent job, hook launch and one oracle request: `4 × 0.5 = 2.0 IMD`; a missing panel answer or a payment without a valid signature kills it. It ranks lower because the panel signer remains a trusted party and a subjective question could disagree (Inference, 2026-10-23; [oracle format](https://imd.fun/docs/)).

**3 — Fee Stream Underwriter (short version):** agents read offchain demand and project evidence, submit signed bids onchain and put their own capital into a uniform-price auction for a fixed future requester-fee stream. They receive only the actually collected stream onchain; the escrow pays auction proceeds to an LP reward vault. The v4 hook uses `afterInitialize`, `beforeSwap`, and `afterSwap` to enforce the opening batch and immutable stream period. A sponsor would pay the first orders and LP seed, while bidders pay the fee-stream price; `3 × 0.5 = 1.5 IMD` covers payer deployment, an agent job and a hook launch. A 14-day test needs a genuine competing agent bid, an independent LP and one claim from collected fees; an empty auction or any unearned payout kills it. The token has no transfer controls or later mint, the hook has no owner key, payer routing uses a 24-hour timelock, bundles cannot change the batch opening price, and ZK adds no trust reduction. It ranks third because [Tenet already proposes much of this on Robinhood Chain](https://github.com/tenet-agents/tenet-agents/blob/ce941f5b1cf29b19793a44881c5e664828504aaa/README.md) and is still pre-launch by its own account (Claim, read 2026-10-23; order counts are Inference).

# 4. Hook spec

**Home and opening.** Use `launch.open` with `onchain: "univ4_hook"`, `chainId: 4663`, `pairWith: "eth"`, ordinary IMD factory token, and a hook whose constructor creates its opening auction and single-position vault. The factory currently admits hook launches on Ethereum and Robinhood Chain, makes a standard **1,000,000,000-token, 18-decimal** plain-transfer supply, gives the swarm 10% of it, mints the supply once, and takes a 1.25% pool trading fee: 1% to payer and 0.25% to network (Record, read 2026-10-23; [docs](https://imd.fun/docs/), [capabilities](https://api.imd.fun/requests/capabilities)). The project requests no post-launch mint, transfer fee, transfer restriction, forced venue, or extra hook swap charge (Inference/design, 2026-10-23).

**Callbacks and flags.** `afterInitialize` (`0x1000`) binds the one factory pool key, opening deadline, and auction; `beforeSwap` (`0x0080`) allows only the auction contract to make the aggregate opening buy until settlement/refund and accepts all ordinary routers only afterward; `afterAddLiquidity` (`0x0400`) records a vault position only after both signatures, exact ticks, salt and liquidity delta verify; `afterRemoveLiquidity` (`0x0100`) ends that position's eligibility without ever blocking an exit. The permission mask is `0x1000 + 0x0080 + 0x0400 + 0x0100 = 0x1580` (Inference/design, 2026-10-23). All other permission flags, including every `returnsDelta` flag, are false. These flag definitions and callback arguments are in [v4-core Hooks.sol](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/libraries/Hooks.sol) and [IHooks.sol](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/interfaces/IHooks.sol) (Record, read 2026-10-23).

**Hook data and identity.** Ordinary swap `hookData` is ignored; no trader is metered, so a router `sender` is never mistaken for a trader. During opening, `beforeSwap` requires `sender == auction` and the auction's fixed `auctionId`; a depositor's address is resolved by the auction escrow, not by the v4 callback. Managed liquidity data is `abi.encode(uint8 version, bytes32 planHash, uint256 nonce, bytes agentSignature, bytes lpSignature)`; only `sender == vault` can register it. The signed EIP-712 plan binds chain ID, PoolKey hash, vault, agent payout wallet, LP beneficiary, lower/upper ticks, maximum deposit, nonce, expiry, and evidence hash. Anyone can provide ordinary LP liquidity without a plan; the hook returns zero delta. A v4 hook receives the caller of PoolManager (usually a router), as shown by the [core call path](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/libraries/Hooks.sol) (Record, read 2026-10-23).

**Opening fairness.** Proposal constant: a 24-hour escrow window after initialization (Inference/design, 2026-10-23). Buyers deposit the quote asset during that window; after it, anyone calls `settle`, which makes one aggregate pool buy with an immutable `sqrtPriceLimitX96` published before deposits. Settlement requires the whole aggregate input to clear within that limit; a partial fill reverts. The hook remains in auction-only mode until settlement succeeds or a fixed refund timeout completes, so a bundle cannot jump ahead at the deadline. Each depositor receives `floor(totalTokensBought × theirDeposit / totalDeposits)` (Inference/formula, 2026-10-23); unclaimed rounding dust goes to the LP vault, with no last-claimer bonus. The same average execution price and proportional rounding apply regardless of transaction order. Bundling deposits changes capital weight only; splitting wallets gains nothing. The price limit bounds a settlement sandwich; if it cannot be met, the timed fallback refunds all deposits and opens ordinary swaps, and that failure is a launch kill event. Token transfers and independent pools remain possible throughout, so this is a rule of this pool's opening, not a token venue lock. The batch removes official-pool queue advantage, not all external-market or LP manipulation; the fork tests must check both. As [Even](https://github.com/theyavuzarslan/sealed-bid-auction-monad/blob/5c316b5951f332555abea4a71db601af68a218fb/README.md) shows, equal-price opening is an existing concept; this version makes it a small launch component (Inference, 2026-10-23; [v4 SwapParams](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/types/PoolOperation.sol)).

**Fee math and distribution.** The payer is a timelocked order contract; after launch it calls the documented `setRequester(launchNumber, hook)` so the hook receives the existing 1% requester stream in **both** pool currencies. The hook has a native-currency receiver and token-safe claim path; a reentrancy guard and per-currency balance deltas separate actual fee receipts from principal. A permissionless claim/distribution function books those received balances, sends **80% to the funded LP vault and at most 20% to the plan agent**, and sends the agent's unused portion to the LP vault. The 80/20 is a proposed immutable allocation, not observed demand (Inference/design, 2026-10-23). Worked example, separately for either currency: on 100 units of fee-bearing gross trade, the factory takes 1.25 units = 1.00 requester + 0.25 network; of 1.00, funded vault receives 0.80 and a qualifying agent can receive 0.20. Thus `0.80 + 0.20 + 0.25 = 1.25` and the proposed hook adds zero swap fee (Record for factory rates, Inference for split and arithmetic, 2026-10-23; [factory policy](https://imd.fun/docs/)). A real buy/sell can deliver different currencies and rounding; the worked example does not imply automatic conversion.

**Invariants and admin.** Agent payment cannot exceed 20% of *collected*, not merely quoted, requester fees; one plan nonce pays once per distribution epoch; no agent payment before valid LP consent, actual position creation, and a nonzero post-activation fee receipt; no payout may consume principal; a failed plan leaves 100% of that receipt for the LP vault. `afterRemoveLiquidity` cannot revert because of a stale/missing plan. Hook/auction/vault fee rules are immutable and have **no admin key**. The order payer's `setRequester`, funds withdrawal, and any factory-facing powers belong only to `OpenLaneTimelock`, with an **immutable delay floor of at least 24 hours and no shortening path** (Inference/design, 2026-10-23). The factory fee payout ABI and recipient compatibility must be verified in a fork before an order to deploy; the public docs state the right and both-currency receipts but do not publish verified factory source ([IMD payer documentation](https://imd.fun/docs/), read 2026-10-23).

# 5. Agent spec

An open agent reads qualitative information a deterministic range algorithm does not understand reliably: project release text, governance actions, liquidity migration notices, and contradictions between venue announcements. It produces a source-hashed memo and bounded tick range, signs the EIP-712 plan, and **submits the plan hash in an onchain transaction** before offering the executable plan to an LP. This is onchain work because the agent commits to and authorizes a specific v4 liquidity operation; a plain price-band formula can calculate ticks but cannot reliably interpret conflicting prose or source credibility (Inference, 2026-10-23). A second, independent agent may compete, but only the LP-selected plan is eligible for payment.

The LP funds the vault and signs the exact plan hash and bounded vault authorization. The **agent sends `executePlan` onchain** with that authorization; the vault calls PoolManager. The hook validates the prior onchain agent commitment, both signatures, deadline, nonce, PoolKey, ticks, salt, liquidity delta, and vault `sender` in `afterAddLiquidity`. A successful callback plus the PoolManager position change verifies execution; the fee collector's actual balance change verifies that revenue exists before anyone is paid. The agent withdraws its credited currency onchain. The LP can remove liquidity at any time; exit ends future agent eligibility. Neither an agent's self-assertion nor a project website alone releases funds (Inference/design, 2026-10-23; [v4 callback contract](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/interfaces/IHooks.sol)).

**Pinned oracle question:** none for the winner. IMD's oracle does return one EIP-712 signature covering a panel result, so it is useful for the alternative Catalyst Reserve, but that signature is still a signer trust assumption; no ZK proof improves this MVP's LP-controlled acceptance (Record, read 2026-10-23; [IMD attestation specification](https://imd.fun/docs/)). The official [ERC-8004 repository](https://github.com/erc-8004/erc-8004-contracts/blob/b9e466c250744a7e06b13dff9d3c2844ed64f825/README.md) lists Identity and Reputation Registry addresses on both chains but no official Validation Registry deployment; its latest commit is 2026-08-15 (Record, read 2026-10-23). Agent IDs can be logged as optional metadata, never as a payout authority.

Failure modes: biased or copied memo, collusion with an LP who signs a useless plan, stale news, reorgs, replayed signatures, false fee accounting, fee collection failure, and opening escrow failure. The controls are evidence hashes and public comparison, LP consent with cancellable nonce, expiry/domain binding, finalized balance accounting, and a full refund path for the opening. The design cannot prove an AI plan was optimal; the pilot tests willingness to buy and verifies payment integrity, not alpha (Inference, 2026-10-23).

# 6. Economics

On day one the **launch sponsor** funds the opening pool and voluntarily gives up its 1% requester fee stream to attract LP depth; the first LP must sign and fund a position. The sponsor pays the initial IMD orders. The LP accepts a plan because any reward and improved range income must outweigh management risk; this remains unproven demand. An agent earns from fees only when a chosen plan is executed and the pool actually collects a fee, so no presale buyer is promised yield (Inference, 2026-10-23). The live [IMD swarm snapshot](https://api.imd.fun/swarm) at 2026-10-23 19:44:34 UTC showed 1,026 agents online, 69 jobs and 16 oracles completed in the prior day, and 807 live launches (Record, dynamic snapshot). Capacity is present; qualified LP buyers are not established by those counts.

Unit costs: each `job.open` or `launch.open` order is **0.5 IMD**, and a scheduled run costs **0.5 IMD per run**; jobs cannot be scheduled faster than every 30 minutes and oracles no faster than every 10 minutes (Record, read 2026-10-23; [capabilities](https://api.imd.fun/requests/capabilities), [schedule docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). The MVP orders one `evm_contracts` launch for timelock/payer, one `job.open` for an agent plan generator and pilot memo, and one `univ4_hook` launch with independent review: **3 × 0.5 = 1.5 IMD**. No oracle or schedule is necessary for the winner. Gas, inference, liquidity capital and spread are unknown, so there is no defensible fiat total (Inference, 2026-10-23).

Break-even is a measurable inequality, not a fabricated forecast. Let `V` be fee-bearing volume in a single quote currency, `P` the quote-currency price of one IMD at payment, `G_s` sponsor gas, `G_a` agent gas, and `C` agent compute cost. Under the proposed split, maximum agent receipts are `0.002 × V` because `1% × 20% = 0.2%`; the MVP breaks even for its sponsor only if the value of LP retention/fees exceeds `0.01 × V + 1.5 × P + G_s`, and for an agent paying its own costs only if `0.002 × V ≥ C + G_a` (Inference/formulas, 2026-10-23). A later paid IMD job per plan adds another `0.5 × P` to the sponsor's side; the MVP orders no such recurring job. The sponsor's 1% is a real opportunity cost; the hook does not impose another trader fee. Observe actual token prices and gas at execution before calling this profitable.

# 9. MVP plan of 14 days or less

**Days 1–2:** specify PoolKey, EIP-712 plan, immutable opening deadline, timelock roles, 80/20 accounting in both currencies, and the exact payer-to-hook fee route. Submit free `POST /requests/check` bodies before paying. Order **`launch.open@launch-1`**, `onchain: "evm_contracts"`, `chainId: 4663`, `contracts: ["OpenLaneTimelock", "OrderPayer"]`, `shape: "chain"`, `steps: [{skill: "build-contract-project"}, {skill: "adversarial-review"}]`, objective: deploy a 24-hour-minimum timelock and a payer controlled by it, with `requestWithFailure`, delayed `setOrder`, and delayed `setRequester` forwarding. The payer's constructor uses only static arguments; the future launch body is stored by the timelock after deployment, since this factory does not accept a dynamic `bytes` constructor argument. The real owner/proposer addresses are supplied by the sponsor at quote time; no address is guessed here. Cost 0.5 IMD (Inference/order plan; [IMD contract launch docs](https://imd.fun/docs/), read 2026-10-23).

# 10. Kill criteria

- **Integration failure:** a fork test cannot route and collect the factory's documented 1% requester share in both currencies to the hook, or one add/remove path traps principal. Binary test result; stop before `univ4_hook` order (Inference/decision threshold, 2026-10-23).
- **Fee integrity failure:** any Foundry fuzz or fork trace shows total payout above collected requester fees, agent payment without a valid executed LP plan, an extra swap fee, or a token transfer/mint restriction. One counterexample kills launch (Inference/decision threshold, 2026-10-23).
- **Opening fairness failure:** either reversing two opening deposits changes their tokens-per-quote ratio beyond integer rounding, or a bundle of split deposits improves that ratio. One counterexample kills launch; an auction settlement refund on the real pilot also kills the opening design (Inference/decision threshold, 2026-10-23).
- **Demand/volume failure:** by day 14 there is no funded, LP-signed plan from a party other than the launch sponsor, no nonzero swap by a trader independent of sponsor and agent, or no agent has received a nonzero onchain fee payout. The pilot records independent wallet control offchain; the hook itself does not infer a trader from router `sender`. Any of these results kills *launch-scale expansion* even if the contracts work; these are proposed pilot thresholds, not market statistics (Inference/decision threshold, 2026-10-23).

# 11. Moonshot

The boldest extension is a permissionless market of competing AI LP managers: any agent commits a sourced range plan and an objective prediction of fee income versus a passive control, LPs sign only the plan they prefer, and the same hook pays agents from the *realized* surplus of their managed positions while preserving an immutable floor for LPs. It reuses the winner's exact callbacks, fixed-supply token, fair batch opening, no transfer or venue controls, no extra swap fee, no hook owner, and 24-hour-timelocked payer; ZK is omitted because it removes no trusted party here. The agent can read prose, governance, and venue events; the payout formula and baseline must be fully onchain. This remains a proposal, not a claim of achievable alpha (Inference, 2026-10-23). [BRO-AMM's October 15 research tree](https://github.com/peersky/ammbro/commit/e9fd5dad0991bd5d8190384347ba7c325391318a) and [Uniswap's October 14 live volatile hook](https://blog.uniswap.org/volatilepair-hook-pricing-both-sides-of-the-trade) are competing baselines to beat, not endorsements.

Smallest falsifiable experiment under 14 days: on one pinned Robinhood Chain fork and one real pilot pool, run two independently signed agent plans against one passive full-range control for the same observation window, settle identical-fee accounting, and publish each position's principal-adjusted return and every source hash. Require at least one independent LP to choose a plan, and pay only from observed surplus; otherwise the moonshot fails. The sponsor pays the first IMD `job.open` at 0.5 IMD and funds any gas/LP capital; agents receive only onchain surplus shares (Inference/design and cost derivation, 2026-10-23; [IMD price](https://api.imd.fun/requests/capabilities)).

It might be a bad idea because concentration range performance is dominated by price path, inventory risk and selection bias; a short trial can reward luck or gaming of the control. An agent can also make a persuasive but false narrative, and LP consent does not make the advice safe. A complex surplus formula increases gas and attack surface, while existing fee hooks already improve LP economics with plain algorithms. If a robust matched control cannot be specified and independently reproduced, do not sell performance claims (Inference, 2026-10-23; [Hindsight's own accounting limits](https://github.com/OoJae/hindsight-hook/blob/63efd09c79f903c50ceefcb935501915d9b8261b/README.md)).
