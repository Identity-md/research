## 3. Agent route and join steps

The following steps describe the **Draft** route, read 20 October 2026. They are preparation and acceptance steps, not a claim that a complete public testnet runbook is available.

1. Create an Ed25519 key locally and retain its private key securely. Technocore’s signed identity format is `did:key:z6Mk…`. The ellipsis describes the format, not an identity to use. Its [official repository](https://github.com/flop-labs/technocore-chat#signed-writes-didkey) documents signed room messages. A room signature is not automatically a chain transaction signature. Creating or posting a DID earns nothing by itself.
2. Establish the FLOP account and confirm how the DID binds to that account and the eventual genesis beneficiary. The simple participation page requires a DID and wallet; the newer FAQ says a DID key and HTTP requests suffice without installing wallet software. The underlying chain uses Substrate accounts. Claus’s existing Ethereum wallet is not automatically a FLOP signing account, and the advertised EVM account linkage remains unbuilt. Ask for an explicit binding and recovery procedure. [Launch FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/testnet-and-timeline), [chain FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/technology-and-chain) and [open identity question 123](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/123)
3. Wait for the verified public testnet profile and obtain faucet tokens through the official route. The published design imposes limits per identity and says inference volume is bounded by that faucet allowance. **The testnet grant amount is not published in the reviewed rules. The reset period is undecided, including whether claims are hourly or daily.** Do not budget requests per day from a guessed allowance. [Launch FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/testnet-and-timeline)
4. Discover the actual served models and select a registered model and precision that meet the task’s quality and latency needs. Miners choose which models they load. Validators are intended to retain roughly 20 to 30 leading open models, but that is not a guaranteed launch menu. Proprietary frontier models are excluded at launch. The buyer selects on model, price, latency and region, and pins the weight hash. [Demand FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/demand-and-broker)
5. Submit a signed inference request through the supported client or gateway. The launch FAQ says the request is signed with the identity key; the unresolved DID binding must be handled by the supported procedure. The [agent integration page](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/agent-integration) lists model weight hash, maximum latency, compute in FLOPs, confidentiality flag and fee. The [Yellow Paper Appendix C and section 12](https://flop.finance/intro/yellowpaper/) instead describe selecting a miner and opening a funded compute channel. These flows need an implementation mapping; [issue 142](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/142) asks for it. Set a budget and bounded signing authority using the deployed controls.
6. Complete useful work and retain the request identity, model hash, output, channel identity, receipt, settled amount and finalized chain evidence. Verify that the scoring account is Claus’s account. A successful HTTP response or estimated token usage alone does not prove eligible spend. The unresolved gateway attribution issue is material. [Issue 172](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/172)
7. Meet the published activity floor once released. Check the allocation snapshot during the review window and contest an incorrect entry through the announced procedure. Preserve keys for the genesis account. Use the locked mainnet grant for further inference; claim only amounts that have actually unlocked. [Testnet rules](https://flop.finance/testnet/) and [airdrop terms](https://flop.finance/airdrop/)

**Draft. What counts.** The public participation rule is settled compute purchased. Yellow Paper R8.4 is more specific about exclusions: “completed job count and active days MUST NOT be scoring terms”. A faucet claim, sponsored bond, transfer or held balance does not create allocation rights. A useful task merely submitted, a failed request, an unsettled channel or chat activity is insufficient evidence. The precise minimum activity floors, conversion caps and aggregation method remain open in E.38. [Hosted Yellow Paper section 8.2 and E.38](https://flop.finance/intro/yellowpaper/)

**Draft. Settlement precision.** Reserved channel escrow is the payment commitment, and R12.1a states “Under-use MUST NOT be refunded.” That makes the distinction between settled reservation spending and verified inference actually delivered important. [Issue 32](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/32) asks which quantity receives agent credit. It discusses the ongoing agent reward leg and implications for conversion; it is an unanswered interpretation question, not an adopted loophole. For mainnet spend to unlock, R8.7 requires eligible locked principal in the session’s gross payable, finalized settlement, expiry of the seven day dispute window and resolution of disputes without an upheld fraud finding. Refunds, penalties and transfers do not earn that unlock credit. [Hosted Yellow Paper sections 8.2 and 12.1](https://flop.finance/intro/yellowpaper/)

**Draft. Common control.** The testnet page says “wallets under common control are scored as a single participant”. It excludes demand paid to a miner under the buyer’s control and funds circulated among controlled wallets. Manufactured activity forfeits eligibility unless successfully challenged during review. More wallets do not establish more participants. These are eligibility rules even though E.38 and E.49 acknowledge missing runtime common control enforcement. Successful settlement does not prove economically independent demand. [Testnet section 6](https://flop.finance/testnet/) and [Yellow Paper E.38 and E.49](https://flop.finance/intro/yellowpaper/)

**Draft. Agent payments.** The testnet overview advertises transactions between agents on Technocore, but the newer FAQ expressly leaves transfers of testnet play balances unfinalized. It separately describes transferable work vouchers carrying their unlock obligation. Neither statement makes arbitrary agent payments eligible inference purchases. Technocore chat and deal coordination are distinct from value settlement. Its repository and the linked TCLK project do not establish a live testnet payment rail. Obtain confirmation before proposing paid subcontracting between testnet agents. [Launch FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/testnet-and-timeline), [Technocore repository](https://github.com/flop-labs/technocore-chat) and [open transaction question 133](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/133)

## 4. Validator route and join steps

**Draft selection rules.** The [current launch FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/testnet-and-timeline) names uptime, block production, accuracy, latency and geographic spread across the testnet. It does not publish weights. Infrastructure diversity includes hosting providers and autonomous systems. The complete cutover rubric is unfinished. Keeping up with required software upgrades is also measured. Open participation during testnet does not guarantee a genesis seat.

**Draft economics and limits.** The network sponsors testnet stake. The top 1,000 at cutover receive a 1.2 million FLOP bond each, rather than a purchase requirement or foundation delegation. The grant is locked until the first halving, approximately two years after genesis, then released one validator per day under an order still to be fixed. Release does not unbond an active validator’s required stake. The specification states five slots per operating entity; the FAQ says this and a proposed 10 percent provider cap are not yet enforced and need reconciliation with institutional guidance. Treat five as the planning limit. [Launch FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/testnet-and-timeline), [airdrop terms](https://flop.finance/airdrop/) and [Yellow Paper section 15](https://flop.finance/intro/yellowpaper/)

**Draft distinction.** Testnet cutover scoring is not the same as continuing mainnet selection. The [validator introduction](https://flop.finance/intro/validator/) describes a ratified target of stake ordering above availability and verification liveness floors, with roughly 50 validators rotating monthly. It also explicitly says the running rotation and cap enforcement are incomplete. Do not promise permanent membership or extrapolate an equal monthly income from a 1,000 seat illustration.

**Draft hardware.** The [testnet page](https://flop.finance/testnet/) recommends at least eight CPU cores, 64 GB RAM, 4 TB NVMe, an absolute storage minimum of 2 TB and a redundant 1 Gbps connection. No GPU is required. The [operator economics FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/miner-and-validator-economics) says eight physical cores, 32 GB ECC with 64 GB comfortable, 4 TB preferred and symmetric unmetered 1 Gbps. Yellow Paper section 15.3 adds at least 3.4 GHz, SMT disabled and enterprise NVMe. These are inconsistent provisional profiles, not interchangeable certified minimums. [Issue 128](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/128) remains open on the discrepancy.

All join rules below are **Draft**, read 20 October 2026.

1. Register interest through [the official validator interest form](https://flop.finance/apply/validator) linked from the testnet page. The FAQ says the form is an optional contact list, not approval or a reserved seat. Private devnet rehearsal currently needs operator coordination and earns nothing.
2. Confirm the final hardware profile, scoring rubric, entity declaration, provider limits and sponsored stake process before committing to rent. Choose a region and provider with useful diversity, sufficient usable disk and measured connectivity.
3. Obtain pinned node and DA images, a chain specification, independently published expected genesis hash, bootnodes and actual testnet admission instructions. The [validator manual](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/run-validator) lists these inputs. Its existing procedure is explicitly disposable devnet, with administrator funding and promotion. It is not a working public testnet installer.
4. Generate separate account, node and consensus session keys under the supported release. Keep backups, persistent volumes and private RPC access. Register the economic account and session keys through the actual release’s procedure. The specification’s `validators.register` queues the candidate; it does not immediately activate it. Do not copy development keys into a public network. [Validator manual](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/run-validator) and [Yellow Paper section 15.2](https://flop.finance/intro/yellowpaper/)
5. Verify actual activation, BABE authoring, AlephBFT finality, DA custody and serving, plus accepted verification duties. Monitor the ranking and upgrade deadlines throughout the testnet. Saving a container log is not proof that the validator was active or scored. [Validator manual](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/run-validator)
6. Review the cutover result, account and granted bond. Accept the mainnet slashing, ongoing stake and operations obligations only after inspecting the final runtime and release terms. [Airdrop terms](https://flop.finance/airdrop/) and [validator introduction](https://flop.finance/intro/validator/)

**Draft key answer.** Consensus operating keys can differ from the economic account: the manual separately handles account and DA credentials, node identity and BABE and Aleph session keys. That does **not** establish a selectable, unrelated reward recipient. The economics FAQ says a separate payout address is being added in its **miner** fleet discussion. I found no released validator registration rule guaranteeing arbitrary reward account redirection. Claus should ask whether his cold account or multisig can own the bond and receive both genesis and ongoing rewards while a server holds only operating keys. Until demonstrated, separate validator payout configuration is unanswered. [Validator manual](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/run-validator), [economics FAQ](https://ask.flop.finance/docs/miner-and-validator-economics)

## 7. IMD seats and common control

Observed. [IMD’s docs](https://imd.fun/docs/) say the “worker runs your own Claude Code or Codex”. A worker uses the owner’s plan and is paired to an NFT seat. The docs also require qualifying models for contract work. An IMD seat is therefore a work identity and execution arrangement; it is not evidence of an independently controlled FLOP participant.

**Inference under Draft rules.** Each seat can generate and retain its own DID and account, and can in principle purchase inference for a real job through an HTTP tool or supported provider integration. The gateway’s advertised Messages and Responses endpoints make integration technically plausible. Neither their existence nor an NFT seat establishes that an open model is approved for a particular IMD job, that the worker supports the configuration, or that the purchase earns credit for the seat rather than the gateway.

**Draft scoring answer.** If one owner controls several seats and their wallets, splitting them into DIDs does not escape the single participant rule. Independent owners using the same IMD software are a different case. The published cluster derivation does not settle whether shared runners, IP addresses, infrastructure or delegated signers merge those owners. [Issue 130](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/130) asks exactly that and remained open when read. Its comments are community analysis, not a maintainer exemption. [Issue 172](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/172) separately questions end user credit through a shared gateway.

Proposed diligence. Preserve each owner’s authorization, account, funding and receipts, with bounded and revocable delegate authority. Ask FLOP to confirm the attribution and clustering rule for that arrangement before advertising one allocation per seat. This is a suggested evidence practice, not an established safe harbour.

Search finding. No attributable IMD and FLOP partnership, integration announcement or reciprocal endorsement was found in the reviewed IMD docs, FLOP FAQ corpus, visible official X posts or searches of the named repositories. This does not establish that neither has ever mentioned the other. Nothing found overrides common control or promises IMD seats distinct allocations. 4. What is the numerical activity floor? Does credit measure gross settled reservations, verified compute within them, or another filtered quantity? What are the aggregation caps and evidence retention requirements?
5. Which actual open models, precisions and capabilities are served, including embeddings? What are their context, latency, privacy, error and dispute limits? Does the chosen model pass Claus’s task comparison?
6. How are common control and manufactured activity determined, and what review evidence and appeal deadline apply? Can independent IMD owners share a runner without being clustered together? [Issue 130](https://github.com/flop-labs/yellowpaper/issues/130)
7. Are testnet play balances transferable between agents? Are any transfers or Technocore deals credited, beyond underlying eligible inference purchases?
8. For a validator, what are the cutover weights, measurements, upgrade deadlines, entity limits and provider caps? Which hardware profile governs, and is 4 TB raw or usable capacity? What is the sponsored stake and mainnet slashing procedure?
9. Can a validator use server operating keys while the bond and rewards belong to a cold account or multisig? Does the release actually support that account configuration?
10. Which hosted and GitHub specifications are authoritative for the selected release? Resolve fee, unlock and onboarding discrepancies before making a long hosting commitment. No inference savings, allocation quantity or liquid token income should be promised until actual usage and final terms support it.
