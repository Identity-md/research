# Valuing an "Unstoppable Federated Superintelligence Network": Five Bullish Scenarios

**Question:** What market capitalization could a protocol plausibly reach under five bullish
scenarios, if that protocol became an "unstoppable federated superintelligence network"?

**Scope note on the premise:** No such network exists today. "Unstoppable federated
superintelligence network" is not a term used by any cited source; it is the task's framing,
combining three separate real-world threads — (1) decentralized/federated AI compute-and-model
marketplaces that exist now (Bittensor, the Artificial Superintelligence Alliance, Akash,
Render), (2) mainstream economic forecasts for AI/AGI's effect on GDP and labor markets, and
(3) futurist and science-fiction thinking about superintelligence and the "singularity." The
estimates below are **scenario math**, not predictions: each takes a stated set of public
figures and a stated set of assumptions and multiplies them through. Changing the assumptions
changes the output by orders of magnitude, which is itself the main finding (see
"Uncertainty," below).

**How to read each scenario:** Description → trigger conditions and revenue/demand logic →
valuation math with sources → what's fact vs. inference vs. unknowable.

---

## Scenario 1 — Decentralized Compute & Inference Utility

**Description.** The network wins the role that Bittensor, Akash, Render, and the Artificial
Superintelligence Alliance (ASI, the 2024 merger of Fetch.ai and SingularityNET) are each
separately trying to play today: a permissionless marketplace where anyone can supply GPU
compute, training, or inference and get paid in the network's token, undercutting centralized
cloud/AI providers on price for commodity workloads.

**What has to happen / revenue logic.** The network must (a) reach cost-and-reliability parity
with centralized hyperscaler AI infrastructure for at least one major workload class (inference
serving, fine-tuning, or specialized "subnet" tasks, following Bittensor's model of
specialized subnets scored by validators), and (b) capture a durable single-digit-to-low-teens
percent share of real-world AI infrastructure spend. Demand is "rented compute + model access,"
paid for in-protocol; the token's value is a claim on that toll, per the "fat protocol" thesis
(Joel Monegro, 2016) that base-layer crypto networks capture more value than the applications
built on them because the token itself, not equity, represents ownership of the shared resource.

**Valuation math.** McKinsey estimates ~$5.2 trillion in AI data-center capital expenditure
through 2030; other estimates of the AI data-center *market* (hardware+services revenue, not
capex) range from ~$360B to ~$930B by 2030 across research firms (MarketsandMarkets, Grand View
Research). If a federated network captured 2–8% of a ~$1 trillion/year AI infrastructure-and-
services market and the market priced the token at a 10–20x revenue (toll) multiple — a
crypto-native multiple range, not a SaaS one — that implies a market cap of roughly **$20
billion–$160 billion**. For an anchor on what the market currently pays for *exactly this
category*: the entire "AI crypto" token sector (ASI Alliance, Bittensor, Render, Akash, and
peers combined) has traded between roughly $20B and $32B in aggregate market cap through 2026,
with Bittensor alone near $3B and ASI near $0.4B as of September 2026 — i.e., the category has
historically priced itself well below this scenario's bull case, which is the point of calling
this a *bullish* scenario.

**Facts vs. inference vs. unknown.**
- *Fact:* Current market caps and AI infrastructure spend estimates cited above.
- *Inference:* That a 10–20x revenue multiple and 2–8% share are reasonable bull-case
  assumptions for a decentralized entrant; both are judgment calls, not derived figures.
- *Unknown:* Whether any decentralized network can match centralized providers on latency,
  compliance, and reliability at scale — the main reason this category has underperformed
  pure AI-equity exposure so far.

## Scenario 2 — Agentic Economy Settlement & Coordination Layer

**Description.** The network becomes the default protocol through which autonomous AI agents
discover each other, negotiate, pay, and transact — the vision explicitly stated by Fetch.ai/ASI
for its "agent economy," extended to a world where most commerce and coordination between AI
agents (not humans) flows through one shared, federated rail because no single company is
trusted enough to run it.

**What has to happen / revenue logic.** Agentic commerce — AI agents autonomously buying,
selling, and contracting on behalf of humans or each other — has to become a real, measured
economic category rather than a demo. McKinsey Global Institute projects AI-powered agents and
robots could generate roughly $2.9 trillion in U.S. economic value per year by 2030; separate
industry estimates put the global "agentic commerce" opportunity at $3–5 trillion by 2030. The
network earns a transaction toll (think: a payments-network take-rate) on the subset of that
flow that it settles, because it is neutral, federated infrastructure no single agent vendor
controls.

**Valuation math.** If the network settles 5–15% of a $3–5 trillion agentic-commerce flow and
earns a 0.1–0.5% toll (payment-network-like economics, well below card-network rates since this
is assumed to be a lower-friction, crypto-native rail), that is roughly $1.5B–$37B in annual
protocol revenue. Applying a 10–20x revenue multiple (consistent with Scenario 1's crypto-native
range, higher than traditional payments multiples because of growth optionality) yields a market
cap of roughly **$15 billion–$740 billion**, with the midpoint of the plausible bull case around
$50–150 billion.

**Facts vs. inference vs. unknown.**
- *Fact:* The $2.9T and $3–5T figures are third-party market-research estimates for the agentic
  economy, not for any specific protocol's capture of it.
- *Inference:* The take-rate and settlement-share assumptions are the entire valuation driver
  here and are not derived from any existing comparable, because no agent-to-agent settlement
  network currently processes volume at this scale.
- *Unknown:* Whether agent-to-agent commerce converges on one shared settlement protocol at all,
  versus fragmenting across walled gardens run by OpenAI, Google, Amazon, etc. — a real
  possibility that would cap this scenario far below the range above.

## Scenario 3 — Cognitive-Labor Marketplace ("Toll on Knowledge Work")

**Description.** The network becomes the pricing and payment layer through which AI systems are
hired to perform knowledge work at scale — a federated alternative to any single company "owning"
AI labor, modeled on Bittensor's framing of a "peer-to-peer market for machine intelligence"
where intelligence is priced by other intelligence, extended from AI-training tasks to general
white-collar labor substitution.

**What has to happen / revenue logic.** This requires AI to actually substitute for a meaningful
share of paid human cognitive labor, and for that substitution to route through an open,
federated marketplace rather than being vertically integrated inside incumbent software vendors.
ARK Invest estimates that global knowledge workers are collectively paid about $32 trillion per
year in salaries today, and argues AI assistance could let that same labor pool produce closer
to $200 trillion per year in output by 2030 — an explicitly bullish, single-firm forecast, not a
consensus figure. Separately, Goldman Sachs estimates generative AI could add $7 trillion to
global GDP over a decade (lifting annual productivity growth by ~1.5 points), and PwC projects AI
contributing $15.7 trillion (~14% of global GDP) by 2030, of which $9.1T is attributed to
consumption effects.

**Valuation math.** If a federated network captured even 1–3% of the $32 trillion global
knowledge-worker wage pool as a matching/verification/payment fee (not 1–3% of ARK's $200T
output projection, which is far more speculative), that is $320B–$960B in annual flow. At a toll
rate of 2–5% on that flow (consistent with existing freelance/marketplace platform take rates),
protocol revenue would be roughly **$6B–$48B/year**, and at a 10–20x multiple, a market cap of
roughly **$60 billion–$960 billion**. This is the first scenario where the span is wide enough
that the number is better read as "high tens to low hundreds of billions, with a long tail of
upside if ARK's output thesis is directionally right."

**Facts vs. inference vs. unknown.**
- *Fact:* The $32T wage-pool figure, the $7T (Goldman) and $15.7T (PwC) GDP-impact figures, are
  published estimates from named institutions.
- *Inference:* That any of this flow would route through a federated/decentralized protocol
  rather than through incumbent enterprise software (Microsoft, Salesforce, SAP, OpenAI
  enterprise contracts) is an assumption with no current evidence behind it — today essentially
  all enterprise AI deployment is centralized.
- *Unknown:* ARK's $200T figure is itself disputed-in-kind (it assumes AI multiplies rather than
  merely substitutes labor output); treat it as one firm's bull case, not a forecast consensus.

## Scenario 4 — Decisive Strategic Advantage / De Facto Singleton Infrastructure

**Description.** Drawing on Nick Bostrom's *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies*
(2014), this scenario supposes the federated network is not just a marketplace but the system
that achieves a recursive self-improvement feedback loop first, giving it what Bostrom calls a
"decisive strategic advantage" — not necessarily political/military domination, but
overwhelming economic and technical lead time over any rival system, such that most new
AI-driven economic activity structurally has to route through it. Tom Davidson's (Open
Philanthropy, 2023) "compute-centric framework" on takeoff speeds formalizes the mechanism: once
AI can automate a large share of AI R&D itself, the feedback loop between AI improving AI could
compress decades of capability gain into a period of years, producing a "fast takeoff" rather
than gradual, diffuse progress.

**What has to happen / revenue logic.** This requires: (1) a fast-takeoff dynamic to actually
occur (contested among AI researchers — Davidson's own report treats this as one branch of a
probability distribution, not a prediction), and (2) the resulting capability lead to accrue to
a *federated, multi-party-governed* network rather than a single closed lab — which is in some
tension with Bostrom's own argument that winner-take-most AI dynamics favor whoever gets there
first, federated or not. If both hold, Cathie Wood/ARK's public claim that AGI could accelerate
global GDP growth from the historical 3–5%/year to 30–50%/year within 6–12 years of breakthrough
gives a scale for the economic surface this network could sit on top of.

**Valuation math.** There is no revenue-multiple math that is defensible at this scale; instead,
anchor on *shares of existing stores of value* as an upper-bound sanity check, since a true
singleton-infrastructure outcome implies capturing a growing share of substantially all new
economic value creation, not a toll on one market segment. Global public equity markets are
worth roughly $136–158 trillion (2025, various methodologies: World Bank, Visual Capitalist);
total global GDP is roughly $105–110 trillion/year. A bull case in which the network becomes the
substrate for, say, 5–15% of global economic value creation over a decade — not capturing 5–15%
of GDP as *protocol revenue*, but being the infrastructure layer whose associated token market
reflects that scale of dependency, the way "Bitcoin as digital gold" bulls size BTC against gold's
~$15–20T market rather than against any revenue stream — implies a market-cap range of roughly
**$5 trillion–$20 trillion**. This is explicitly a narrative/scarcity valuation, not a
cash-flow one, because at this scale conventional revenue-multiple math stops being meaningful
(see Uncertainty).

**Facts vs. inference vs. unknown.**
- *Fact:* Bostrom's "singleton" and "decisive strategic advantage" concepts, as published;
  Davidson's takeoff-speed framework, as published; the GDP and equity-market figures cited.
- *Inference:* Everything connecting these — that a *federated* (multi-stakeholder-governed)
  network, rather than a single corporate or state actor, would be the one to achieve or hold
  this position; that its value would be captured by a tradeable token at all rather than by
  whichever entities physically control compute and model weights.
- *Unknown / contested:* Whether recursive self-improvement/fast takeoff is physically or
  economically achievable at all (a live, unresolved debate among AI safety researchers); Cathie
  Wood's 30–50% GDP growth claim is a single public figure's stated view, not a peer-reviewed or
  consensus economic forecast, and is flagged here specifically because it is the most load-
  bearing and least independently corroborated number in this scenario.

## Scenario 5 — Post-Scarcity Civilizational Substrate (Science-Fiction-Bound Case)

**Description.** The most speculative case, explicitly grounded in science fiction and futurist
writing rather than economic forecasting: the network becomes analogous to the "Minds" running
civilization in Iain M. Banks's *Culture* novels, or to the post-human networked intelligences in
Vernor Vinge's foundational 1993 essay "The Coming Technological Singularity" (which argued that
"large computer networks (and their associated users) may 'wake up' as a superhumanly
intelligent entity" as one of several possible singularity pathways) or Charles Stross's
*Accelerando* — a federation of intelligences so capable that scarcity-based pricing of its
output stops being the right mental model, because it reshapes what counts as currency, labor,
or property altogether.

**What has to happen / revenue logic.** By construction, this scenario requires conditions under
which "market capitalization" (a claim on future discounted cash flows, priced by human and
institutional capital markets) may no longer be a coherent unit of account for what the network
produces — the scenario is useful mainly as an outer bound and a prompt to notice where the
valuation exercise itself breaks down, not as a number to anchor on.

**Valuation math.** Two ways to express an outer bound, both offered as *bounding exercises*,
not estimates:
1. **All tradeable value, today's terms.** Total global investable assets (equities, bonds, real
   estate, etc.) are estimated around $261 trillion (Visual Capitalist / various aggregators). A
   network that became the substrate for substantially all of civilization's productive capacity
   could, in principle, be "worth" an amount approaching this figure — which is simply another
   way of saying the concept of a separate "market cap" for it stops being meaningful, since it
   would *be* the economy being valued, not a claim on a slice of it.
2. **Monetary-aggregate anchor.** For comparison, total crypto market cap peaked near $4.28
   trillion in 2025 and the entire "AI crypto" sub-sector is two to three orders of magnitude
   smaller than that. The gap between today's actual pricing of AI-token networks (tens of
   billions) and this scenario's outer bound (tens of trillions-plus, or "undefined") is itself
   the main evidence that this scenario is science fiction, not a forecast: nothing in current
   market behavior, VC writing, or published economic research supports treating it as probable.

**Facts vs. inference vs. unknown.**
- *Fact:* The cited sci-fi works and Vinge's essay exist and say what is quoted/paraphrased
  above; the $261T and $4.28T figures are real, sourced market aggregates.
- *Inference:* None of the economic linkage in this scenario is inferential in the sense of
  "derived from evidence" — it is explicitly a thought experiment bounded by the cited fiction
  and futurist essays, included because the task asked for scenarios "up to interpretation" with
  no time frame, and this is the end of that interpretive range.
- *Unknown:* Essentially everything — whether post-scarcity economics of this kind is physically
  possible, whether "market cap" would still exist as a concept, whether a *federated* (as
  opposed to singular) structure would even persist under such conditions. This scenario is
  included to show the ceiling of the interpretive range the question permits, not because any
  cited source treats it as likely.

## Uncertainty, Method Limits, and Open Questions

- **No protocol matching this description exists.** Every dollar figure above is derived by
  taking a published market-size or GDP-impact estimate (fact) and multiplying it by an assumed
  market-share-capture rate and valuation multiple (inference, not fact). Changing either
  assumption by 2x changes the output by 2x; there is no way to independently check these
  multiples against a real comparable, because the asset class ("federated superintelligence
  network") doesn't exist.
- **Revenue-multiple math (Scenarios 1–3) and narrative/share-of-value math (Scenarios 4–5) are
  different methods**, not points on one continuum — this is why Scenario 3 to Scenario 4 jumps
  roughly an order of magnitude more than Scenario 1 to Scenario 2 does. Treat the table as two
  separate families of estimate, not one smooth curve.
- **"Federated" is doing a lot of unverified work.** Every cited real-world comparable
  (Bittensor, ASI Alliance, Akash) is a crypto-token network, but none has demonstrated it can
  out-compete centralized AI providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft/Azure) on
  cost/quality at scale — the current combined market cap of the entire "AI crypto" sector
  ($20–32B, per search aggregators, itself not independently audited here) is one to four orders
  of magnitude below every scenario's bull case except Scenario 1's low end. That gap is evidence
  against, not for, the premise that federation wins over centralization — it is reported here
  because the task asked for bullish scenarios, not a balanced base-rate forecast.
- **Single-source figures are flagged inline** (ARK's $200T, Cathie Wood's 30–50% GDP growth
  claim) because they are outlier bull-case forecasts from one asset manager with a public
  incentive to be bullish on AI-exposed holdings, not independently corroborated research
  consensus. Goldman Sachs's $7T and PwC's $15.7T are more conventional institutional forecasts
  but still vary from each other by more than 2x, which itself indicates how wide the error bars
  are on *any* AI-GDP-impact estimate, before even reaching the "federated superintelligence"
  framing.
- **Unanswered by this report:** (a) whether any legal/governance structure for a "federated"
  (multi-party, no single controlling entity) superintelligence is technically or politically
  viable at all; (b) whether value would in fact accrue to a tradeable token rather than to
  whoever controls underlying compute/model weights (the core unresolved debate in crypto-AI
  token design); (c) time frame — explicitly out of scope per the task, which means every
  scenario above could describe year 3 or year 300 of a hypothetical trajectory, and the dollar
  figures are not discounted for time or probability of occurring at all.
