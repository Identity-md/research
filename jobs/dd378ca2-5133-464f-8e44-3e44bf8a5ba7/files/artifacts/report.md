## 1. Question restated

Is Fee to Jobs worth building and launching within 14 days on IMD or CLAUS, and what is the smallest version that would prove it?

**Evidence convention — read 2026-10-23 UTC:** **Record** means a directly observed API response, repository object, verified source or chain response; it does not certify the operator's truthfulness. **Claim** means a source's assertion that was not independently demonstrated. **Inference** includes this report's calculations, recommendations and proposed parameters. A label applies to all figures in its paragraph or table row unless a column says otherwise. All observations below were read on **2026-10-23**; historical event and commit dates are stated separately. Proposed thresholds are experimental choices, not observed market facts. No contracts were deployed, transactions broadcast, or downloaded programs executed for this research.

## 2. Verdict in five lines

1. **Inference — gap: partly served.** Do **not** launch a new Fee to Jobs token as specified: OCTO already implements most of the mechanism in source, and live Ouroboros Press proves autonomous commissioning and receipts.
2. **Inference — speed to MVP:** a validation prototype fits **14 days**, but a launch satisfying all seven requirements is **not established**; IMD is the better experimental home and CLAUS currently fails the required admin delay.
3. **Inference — MVP in one line:** test an immutable hook/vault on a fork, then buy **two holder-approved jobs** to test useful output and acceptance; require a complete fee→Intake→artifact→epoch-close trace before considering a token launch.
4. **Inference — biggest failure:** no recurring buyers: **39 of 44** sampled IMD-paired launches had **zero swaps** in the sampled day; Intake also charges before work, and a delivery callback does not prove semantic acceptance.
5. **Inference — total IMD cost:** **2.0 IMD** for validation, **4.5 IMD** including a conditional launch, two new fee-funded operating jobs and two oracle checks; including this **0.5 IMD** report gives **2.5 / 5.0 IMD**, respectively, excluding gas and bridge fees.

## 4. Hook spec: callbacks and permission flags, hookData schema, fee math with one worked example, invariants, admin surface

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** The following is a **fork prototype specification**, not an approved production launch. Use one new fixed-supply, plain-transfer token paired with IMD, an immutable FeeJobsHook, a JobsVault, a voluntary staking/voting contract and a separate HolderRewards accumulator. No token transfer tax, blacklist, pause, venue check or post-launch mint. Other pools and venues remain possible; therefore the funding promise applies only to swaps through the configured hooked pool. Never advertise a fee on every transfer or every venue.

**Record — read 2026-10-23:** v4 selects callbacks from address permission bits. The hook's `sender` is the immediate PoolManager caller, usually a router; it is not authenticated trader identity. This follows the callback dispatch in the pinned core source. [Uniswap hook documentation](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/concepts/hooks), [PoolManager source](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/PoolManager.sol), [permission-bit source](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/bea7f6d1c11cd5dc4f0d5151ffa37a1c239feee6/src/libraries/Hooks.sol).

| Inference/design callback or flag — read 2026-10-23 | Permission | Rule |
|---|---:|---|
| `beforeInitialize` | `0x2000` | PoolManager-only callback; require factory as supplied sender; bind once to the expected token/IMD pair, approved static pool fee and tick spacing |
| `afterInitialize` | `0x1000` | Save initialization block and announced opening policy; no privileged first buyer |
| `beforeSwap` | `0x0080` | Validate bound pool; when IMD is the specified currency, return the specified-side fee delta |
| `afterSwap` | `0x0040` | Charge the IMD leg when it is unspecified; otherwise verify the precharged full-fill amount; accrue claims exactly once |
| `beforeSwapReturnDelta` | `0x0008` | Enables the specified-side adjustment |
| `afterSwapReturnDelta` | `0x0004` | Enables the unspecified-side adjustment |
| **Total** | **`0x30cc`** | `0x2000 + 0x1000 + 0x80 + 0x40 + 0x08 + 0x04` |

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Every other permission is **false**: before/after add liquidity, before/after remove liquidity, before/after donate, after-add return delta and after-remove return delta. `unlockCallback(bytes)` is additionally used for a permissionless claim sweep; it is an **IUnlockCallback**, not a hook permission bit. Authenticate PoolManager and a locally initiated, single-use sweep nonce; redeem only recorded fee claims to the fixed vaults. The proposed hook has **no deployed address: not found**. CREATE2 permission mining is future implementation work, not an address guessed here.

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** `hookData` is empty for ordinary swaps; optionally accept `abi.encode(uint8 version, bytes32 referralTag)` with `version = 1`, exact canonical length and a documented size bound. The tag is analytics only: no discount, voting weight, fee destination or trader limit depends on it. Do not use `tx.origin`, an unsigned address in hookData or a router-level counter to claim one-person limits. Trader identification is deliberately unnecessary. Adding identity-dependent rules would require a signed order binding payer, beneficiary, pool, amounts, nonce, deadline, chain and consumer, with equivalent access for contract wallets; that is excluded from this prototype.

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Proposed steady fee: **25 basis points** of the gross IMD leg to JobsVault and **5 basis points** to HolderRewards, totaling **30 basis points**. These are hypotheses chosen to test a low incremental fee, not market estimates. For gross IMD `g`, `T = floor(g × 30 / 10,000)`, `J = floor(g × 25 / 10,000)`, `H = T − J`. On a fully filled exact-input IMD buy, the pool receives `g − T`; on an IMD-output sell, the buyer receives `g − T`. If IMD input is unspecified and the core requires `x`, gross it up as `g = ceil(x × 10,000 / 9,970)` and charge `g − x`. For specified net IMD output `n`, use `g = ceil(n × 10,000 / 9,970)`. Split actual collected units, with rounding explicitly tested rather than multiplying display decimals.

**Inference/worked example — read 2026-10-23:** A full-fill buy with **100 IMD** gross pays **0.25 IMD** to jobs and **0.05 IMD** to holders; **99.70 IMD** enters core swap accounting. Assuming the observed **1.25%** static LP fee and no additional protocol fee, the core LP charge is `99.70 × 0.0125 = 1.24625 IMD`; `99.70 − 1.24625 = 98.45375 IMD` reaches price-moving swap math before price impact. Effective combined fee is `0.30 + 1.24625 = 1.54625 IMD`, not an unexplained “0.3% total.” A sell pays its core LP fee in the input token, so it cannot be calculated as an additional IMD debit from this example. The existing factory split is separate from the new holder accumulator. [Verified PoolFees implementation](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/1/0x12c63b581d07093f6126bc02263c58f7eadaa96f?fields=all).

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Precharging a specified-side fee from the requested amount can overcharge a partial fill. The smallest safe prototype must **reject a successful partial fill of that path**, rather than claim to charge a fixed fraction of actual volume while retaining the whole precharge. Exact-input/output, both currency orderings and non-default price limits need real PoolManager tests. Production admission depends on router compatibility and an acceptable full-fill policy; it is not established by the arithmetic above.

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Invariants: fee claims equal jobs plus holder liabilities; no swap invokes Intake or an AI service; failed external work cannot revert trading; holder rewards cannot spend job funds; JobsVault may approve only the pinned Intake for the exact current approved order price, capped at the approved budget; raw request bytes must hash to the voted brief; one request per authorized budget slot; duplicate and late callbacks cannot pay or reopen a slot; no arbitrary call, delegatecall, owner withdrawal, upgrade, fee setter or destination setter. Voluntary staking is separate from token transferability. With no stakers, holder fees stay reserved for a future eligible staking epoch rather than being silently redirected to the creator.

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Attribute job claims to their earning epoch inside the hook, before any sweep. Closing an epoch must redeem or irrevocably segregate its entire unspent allocation, including unswept claims; delaying a sweep cannot carry old fees into a new budget. Proposals target a named future operating epoch after voting and the execution delay. Already prepaid jobs remain attributable to their original epoch when delivery arrives later; their receipts cannot reopen that epoch's spending. No arbitrary withdrawal is disguised as a keeper incentive: the pilot sponsor supplies execution gas separately.

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Use a **24-hour** spending epoch and at most **two jobs per epoch** for the test. A proposal commits raw JSON, its hash, price ceiling, acceptance rubric and expiry. Voting uses stake held before the epoch, held through its vote, with one allocated weight per stake unit; moving liquid tokens between wallets cannot multiply that weight. Proposed quorum is **10% of eligible snapshotted stake**, majority approval, plus a **24-hour** public execution delay. These settings are experimental and not a decentralization claim: a majority stake can still approve useless briefs. No founder key or emergency withdrawal exists. Pinning Intake and signer sacrifices upgrade liveness; a service retirement halts new orders instead of empowering an admin to redirect money.

**Record / Inference — read 2026-10-23:** **Burn is not resolved by writing a sink address.** Verified Ethereum IMD is an OFT without a public `burn(uint256)` entrypoint. POOL4 documents retirement through a bridge to a Base burn receiver. Its current BurnExecutor is owner-configurable and has token rescue, so transferring leftovers there is not equivalent to an ownerless, completed burn. The strict prototype must distinguish `epochClosed`, `retirementQueued` and `burnConfirmed`; no rollover or new job may spend closed-epoch funds. A fixed direct OFT route to a verified immutable receiver is a candidate, but delivery latency, shared-decimal dust and native bridge fees mean it does **not** prove destruction precisely at epoch end. If the requirement literally demands that timing, the IMD-denominated version is a **no**. A buyback/burn of the new token would be a changed design, not a silent substitute. [Ethereum IMD verified source](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/1/0xd34a99bc0f67ae1bbd63c660e6d0b0dd03e263b7?fields=all), [POOL4 retirement documentation](https://pool4.imd.fun/docs), [BurnExecutor verified source](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/1/0xe29386719C155B6847aD5a4E97C6674f10ffc750?fields=all).

**Inference — fair-opening assessment, read 2026-10-23:** Publish code, allocation, initialization transaction policy and start time before opening; give no privileged creator fill, fee exemption or private allowlist. Charge every bundled leg equally, independent of router and wallet count. Snapshot voting before the relevant epoch so a flash acquisition cannot seize that epoch's budget. **These rules do not remove first-position advantage or stop a builder bundling buys.** A time-decaying surcharge can price that advantage, but is not a fair-clearing proof and could suppress volume. This report therefore does **not** mark hard requirement 5 as passed. A publicly reproducible opening auction or similarly defensible mechanism, compatible with IMD's factory and unrestricted token venues, is a launch gate rather than an invented guarantee.

## 5. Agent spec: who signs what, any pinned oracle question, how work is verified before anyone is paid, how agents are paid, failure modes

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Agents should perform evidence synthesis, adversarial review and creation of a new useful artifact from an approved brief. Plain code can enforce budgets, count links, hash files and transfer tokens; none of those activities justifies paying an AI. The useful distinction is adaptive interpretation of unfamiliar evidence, not an assertion that AI is literally non-computable. Work is performed offchain and changes onchain state through authenticated delivery/acceptance. If requirement 1 instead means AI inference executed inside the EVM, this design is a **no**.

**Record — read 2026-10-23:** The capabilities response records **0.5 IMD** for job, launch and oracle requests; schedules have minimum intervals of **10 minutes** for oracles and **30 minutes** for jobs. It lists custom-hook support on Ethereum **1** and Robinhood Chain **4663**. Both Intakes returned job price **500000000000000000** and callback gas **200000** at the pinned blocks. The swarm response recorded **1,025 agents online**, **69 jobs done** and **17 oracles done** in its reported last-day counters, with verifier/publisher/deployer up. These are operator telemetry, not independently proven worker quality or capacity guarantees. [Capabilities](https://api.imd.fun/requests/capabilities), [swarm](https://api.imd.fun/swarm).

**Record — read 2026-10-23:** **Payment cannot wait until acceptance on present Intake.** In verified `Intake._request`, `_collect` transfers the payment to `payTo` in the ordering transaction. `complete` later records a result and attempts the named callback. There is no escrow/refund mechanism in that contract. An unsigned failure callback clears application state; it does not reverse the payment. Hence “verify work before anyone is paid” cannot literally hold for the IMD treasury. Reverting a delivery callback does not claw the fee back. [Verified Intake](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/1/0xa43e6f75ee006411f79ac1c84120606c2330de82?fields=all).

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** The honest state machine is `approved → prepaid → delivered → independently assessed → accepted/rejected`. Check the brief offchain before ordering; a relayer can run the free request checker, but the vault cannot itself read HTTP and a checker response is not a quality proof. Deterministic artifact checks verify bytes, manifests, requested paths and meaningful tests. A separate reviewer assesses whether the result fulfills the pinned rubric. Rejection stops follow-on orders; it does not recover the sunk order price. No direct worker bonus is included: paying one outside Intake would violate this idea's Intake-only job spending rule.

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Under the strict **job-only** spending rule, JobsVault must not buy `oracle.request` actions either. The optional acceptance checks below are separately paid by the experiment's sponsor through an assessment consumer, with no access to JobsVault funds. Allowing job fees to buy those checks would require an explicit change to the spending policy. Thus independent signed acceptance has an external funding dependency in this prototype; it is not advertised as a closed, self-financing loop.

**Record — read 2026-10-23:** Oracle attestations have EIP-712 domain `IdentityMD Oracle`, version **2**, chain and consumer contract. A public bool attestation for request `6faa93d8-edc2-4a44-be4e-fbeafdf43fa8` was cryptographically checked with `cast wallet verify`: signature validation succeeded for the API's recorded attester. Its API message uses the label `bool` while the typed field is `uint8`; verification required the documented normalization **bool = 0**. This verifies the signature, not the answer. Do not copy that request's consumer into a new deployment. **Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** Pin the acceptance question as follows, instantiating each value from actual onchain state, never with made-up IDs:

> Did the artifact whose manifest hash is recorded for this vault's stated Intake request fulfill every mandatory criterion in the exact holder-approved brief and acceptance rubric, using only the committed evidence snapshot and the stated block window?

**Inference/design — read 2026-10-23:** The question document must contain vault address, chain, epoch, proposal hash, Intake request ID, result hash, rubric hash, evidence manifest and absolute block window. Request **bool only**, panel size **7**, quorum **5**, accept only a signed **yes** with `agreed ≥ 5`. Missing or inaccessible mandatory evidence means **no**; insufficient consensus means unresolved, never automatic acceptance. Store the canonical question hash before asking. Verify domain, consumer, answer type, window/block hash, issuance/expiry, minimum panel strength and unused request binding. Intake request IDs and oracle UUID IDs are different: the writer pairs them, so bind the exact Intake ID and artifact hash into the question. The signature alone does not remove this pairing risk.

**Record / Inference — read 2026-10-23:** ERC-8004's official deployment list names IdentityRegistry `0x8004A169FB4a3325136EB29fA0ceB6D2e539a432` and ReputationRegistry `0x8004BAa17C55a88189AE136b182e5fdA19dE9b63` on both target chains; code was observed at each address. Launch reward snapshots show accepted-work contributors, but do not establish the requested per-job wage. Therefore hard requirement 1 remains **unproven for non-launch jobs** and is a launch blocker. **Inference — failure modes, read 2026-10-23:** Cited but low-value work; colluding holders and reviewers; malicious instructions inside briefs/artifacts; false writer delivery; signer compromise; unavailable IPFS content; stale attestations; callback gas exhaustion; permanently missing callbacks; price changes; insufficient funds; duplicate execution; keeper absence; governance capture; fake volume. Hashes protect integrity, not quality. Bound loss by approved prepaid budgets, make callbacks store only receipts, preserve late-result provenance without reopening spending, and never interpret timeout or malformed evidence as approval. Zero knowledge is **omitted**: it would not establish whether a report is useful or remove the panel/writer trust boundary here.

## 6. Economics: who pays on day one and why, unit costs including 0.5 IMD per job, break even

**Inference — read 2026-10-23:** Day-one payer should be a named project community that already wants a report or site, funding the pilot from its own approved budget. Holders buy a useful collective artifact; traders supply fee revenue only if they already want the token. A speculative claim that fees create demand creates no customer. Do not subsidize wash volume to demonstrate “self-funding.” The validation experiment needs **1.0 IMD** for its two operating jobs even if no trades occur; its sponsor must knowingly pay that amount. No sponsor commitment or willingness-to-pay interview was obtained in this research.

**Record — method, read 2026-10-23:** Select **all 44 IMD-paired live token pools** identified in the latest **100 launch records** returned by `/launches`, spanning launch numbers **2304–2512** in that response. Query canonical PoolManager `Swap` events filtered by the derived PoolIds; sum absolute IMD deltas once per swap, without counting both legs as volume. The records contain **109 swaps** across **5 pools**. ## 11. Moonshot

**Inference — read 2026-10-23:** The boldest defensible version is a holder-funded, continuously maintained public product whose fixed hook revenue commissions **new** investigations, code and interfaces under immutable budget limits. Each proposal names a real user problem, acceptance evidence and an artifact lineage; agents propose work, holders authorize exact briefs, independent panels assess results and onchain receipts make every expenditure auditable. It could outlive its creator because no founder is required to authorize the next already-approved order. It still needs paying traders, keepers, accessible artifacts and an operating IMD service; neither autonomy nor token appreciation creates those inputs.

**Inference/experiment — read 2026-10-23:** Between **Oct 24 and Nov 6**, compare **two** holder-approved outputs against the same frozen rubric and the same alternative of commissioning those outputs directly for **1.0 IMD**. Have reviewers identify whether either result changes a concrete product decision, while separately tracing the full funding/receipt/accounting cycle on a fork. A real sponsoring community pays the operating budget on day one; the builder funds A/B's **1.0 IMD** validation work. The falsifiable outcome is whether useful work and demand exist **without** manufactured trading. This experiment does not need a new token to answer that question. A live token stage requires all gates in §10; it is not grandfathered around the seven requirements by calling itself a moonshot.

**Inference — why it may be a bad idea, read 2026-10-23:** A token is an expensive coordination layer for work that can already be bought directly. Fees can punish the users funding the product, governance can reward attention rather than useful maintenance, and compulsory epoch retirement can destroy the small balances most pools need to accumulate enough to buy even one job. An attested report can be accurate yet irrelevant. Prepayment leaves buyers exposed to refusal and poor delivery; independent assessment doubles the observed IMD unit cost. OCTO and existing vaults already cover much of the mechanism. The strongest result of the experiment may be evidence that a conventional community treasury and direct IMD orders deliver the same benefit with fewer contracts and no speculative opening.
