# Five charities for a recurring crypto donation split

Research date: **27 October 2026**. Financial periods are identified below; current web pages can contain older information.

**Recommendation:** prioritize MSF USA and Americares for broad humanitarian response, Direct Relief for medical logistics, All Hands & Hearts for water and recovery, and World Central Kitchen for emergency food. This is a ranked research shortlist, **not five fully cleared recipients**: MSF USA and All Hands & Hearts disclose litigation, Direct Relief has accepted oil-sector support, and permanent donation-wallet verification remains unresolved for all five. If “no pending litigation” and “no controversial funding” are absolute vetoes rather than scored criteria, the evidence does **not** support approving this five-way split as presented.

Scores are this report’s judgments, not evaluator ratings or measured impact estimates. Each criterion receives equal weight, 1–10; totals are sums, with no separate category bonus. Category breadth informs effectiveness and capacity. A one-point difference is not decision-grade precision: ranks 1–4 are close, and different treatment of the disclosed exceptions could reorder them.

**Score key, in the brief’s order:** E effectiveness; N non-political bias/independence; P program expense ratio; C crypto acceptance; T track record/governance; F financial transparency; L legal standing; O operational capacity; D funding diversity/ethical sources; V wallet verification. In the Notes column, vectors follow **E/N/P/C/T/F/L/O/D/V**. Strong documented performance generally earns 8–10, partial evidence 5–7, and important failures or missing verification 1–4. An unknown is not a clean bill of health. No reviewed evidence established partisan funding by a shortlisted entity; this was a document review, not a comprehensive political-expenditure audit.

**Why each made the cut — three sentences each**

**1. MSF USA.** Its medical teams, [therapeutic feeding programs](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/what-we-do/medical-issues/malnutrition) and water/sanitation interventions offer the strongest combined coverage of the five categories, and its funding model supports independent responses across more than 70 countries. [Haiti’s 2010 earthquake response](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/what-we-do/focus/haiti-ten-years-after-earthquake) included over 358,000 patients treated in ten months, while its [2023 Türkiye/Syria response](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/latest/turkey-earthquakes-impact-msfs-response) included drinking water, sanitation and meals; these are reported outputs, not attributable lives saved per donated dollar. Its [logistics description](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/what-we-do/focus/natural-disaster-response) supports aircraft dispatch within 24 hours, and [Charity Navigator rates the US entity 96%, four stars](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/133433452), but the lawsuits below prevent a clean legal-screen result.

**2. Americares.** It combines medicine distribution, clinical services, WASH and disaster preparedness, reporting [30 emergency responses in 22 countries and over nine million prescriptions distributed in FY2025](https://www.americares.org/newsroom/publications/2025-annual-report/). Its [Haiyan response](https://www.americares.org/emergency-program/philippines-typhoon-haiyan/) placed a team on the ground within 72 hours, and its [Chile response record](https://www.americares.org/news/2010-0227-americares-aiding-earthquake-survivors-in-chile/) also documents the Haiti earthquake and Asian tsunami responses. The [2022 WHO-team certification announcement](https://www.americares.org/news/2022-0728-achieve-world-health-orgnization-emergency-medical-team-certification/) describes international deployment capability within 72 hours, and [Charity Navigator gives 95%, four stars](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/061008595), with financial-accounting and funding caveats below.

**3. Direct Relief.** Its comparative advantage is supplying established health providers rather than building an entirely new operation after each disaster: FY2025 reporting records [29,657 deliveries to providers in 92 countries and 311.9 million defined daily doses](https://www.directrelief.org/about/charity-rating/). Its [Haiti self-report, archived by GiveWell](https://www.givewell.org/files/DisasterRelief/Direct%20Relief%20International/Haiti%206-Month%20Report.pdf), describes an airlift departing within 72 hours, and its [Haiyan record](https://www.directrelief.org/emergency/typhoon-haiyan/) describes mobilization within 24 hours, establishing two major-crisis responses without implying universal delivery deadlines or a GiveWell endorsement. Its [100%, four-star Charity Navigator rating](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/951831116) and no-government-funding policy are positives, but its historical oil-sector support materially lowers this brief-specific ranking.

**4. All Hands & Hearts.** It adds practical rebuilding and clean-water capacity: its [2025 review](https://allhandsandhearts.org/2025-year-in-review) describes a Myanmar earthquake response mobilized from Thailand within 24 hours, subsequent borehole/filter work, and Hurricane Helene/Milton recovery. The same review reports work spanning 30 countries over its history; the [2017 merger history](https://www.allhandsandhearts.org/about-us/) explains why predecessor experience and current-brand age differ, and its [leadership and board structure](https://allhandsandhearts.org/about-us/our-team) supports an inference of institutional capacity beyond one founder. Its [98%, four-star Charity Navigator rating](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/203414952) and donor-diversity evidence are strong, while litigation and safeguarding history require a substantial legal/governance discount.

**5. World Central Kitchen.** WCK supplies the clearest emergency-food component, reporting [more than 130 million meals in 2025](https://wck.org/resources/), although meal counts do not establish long-term nutrition outcomes or a universal cost per meal. Its [history](https://wck.org/story/) documents Haiti’s 2010 earthquake and the 2017 Harvey/Maria responses, and its [donation page](https://wck.org/donate/) describes mobilization within hours. Its [100%, four-star Charity Navigator rating](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/273521132) and distributed restaurant/response network support inclusion, with the leadership, related-party and legal qualifications below.

**Evidence behind the remaining scores, red flags and unanswered questions**

- **Legal standing is distinct from absence of lawsuits.** All five US entities are identified as 501(c)(3) nonprofits by their linked Charity Navigator profiles, with matching EINs and public financial reporting. MSF USA’s FY2025 audit, note 14, explicitly identifies it as defendant in several ordinary-course lawsuits; All Hands & Hearts’ note 9 says it is involved in litigation; WCK’s note 12 discloses ordinary-course claims/legal matters. Management’s expectation of no material financial effect does **not** establish no pending litigation, and no post-audit case-resolution confirmation was obtained. The reviewed Direct Relief and Americares audits did not identify litigation; neither absence of disclosure nor this limited public-source review certifies that no case is pending.

- **MSF: independence and ethical funding are unusually well documented, with limits.** Its [US donor FAQ](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/get-involved/ways-to-give) says it has not solicited or received US government funding since 2002; the movement reports [98% private operating funding in 2025](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/who-we-are/finances-reporting-accountability). Its [corporate acceptance policy](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/get-involved/ways-to-give/corporate-giving/corporate-gift-acceptance-policy) excludes arms and oil/gas/extractive industries, among others, but does not explicitly list gambling; enforcement and the absence of any donor above 20% were not established. MSF openly conducts [humanitarian advocacy](https://www.msf.org/speakingout), treated here as within the brief’s humanitarian-scope allowance; it would fail a stricter requirement for no advocacy whatsoever. Its [2026 Gaza explanation](https://www.doctorswithoutborders.org/your-questions-about-our-work-gaza-answered) also describes restrictions on importing supplies and international staff entry: global capacity does not override local access restrictions.

- **Americares: government exposure and concentration remain relevant.** Its FY2025 audit reports $3.139 million in government grants against $95.639 million in cash/financial-asset contributions: about **3.18% of those two funding streams combined**, versus about **0.17% of total revenue including donated products**. These figures suggest limited aggregate dependence, not that individual government-funded projects are insulated from cancellation. Its [September 2026 announcement](https://www.americares.org/news/americares-announces-record-50-million-gift-to-expand-access-to-lifesaving-medicines/) describes a $50 million family gift **over five years**, partly for endowment; it must not be counted as $50 million of annual operating income. No complete donor-by-donor cash/in-kind concentration schedule or comprehensive arms/fossil-fuel/gambling exclusion policy was established.

- **Direct Relief: a concrete ethical-funding mismatch.** Its [2013 Haiyan update](https://www.directrelief.org/2013/12/typhoon-haiyan-response-one-month-update/) names **Aera Energy LLC and Yorba Oil Company, Ltd.** among supporters providing goods and/or money; this is evidence of historical oil-sector support, not proof of a current grant or its size. It therefore does not satisfy a lifetime prohibition on fossil-fuel funding. Its [finance policy](https://www.directrelief.org/about/finance/) reports zero government funding and assistance without regard to politics, while no complete current donor-concentration ceiling or gambling/arms funding clearance was established. “Zero donated funds spent on fundraising” reflects legacy investment earnings funding fundraising costs; those costs still exist.

- **All Hands & Hearts: diversity evidence is stronger than most, but not comprehensive.** Its FY2025 audit says no donor supplied over 10% of contributions/grants revenue in either 2025 or 2024; this is useful evidence, although narrower than a beneficial-donor analysis combining every cash and in-kind source. Its [FY2025 public Form 990](https://allhandsandhearts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/All-Hands-and-Hearts-Smart-Response-2024-990-PDC.pdf) reports no government-grant revenue; that does not establish absence of government-origin funding passed through partners. The organization has [acknowledged historical sexual-harassment incidents and commissioned a policy review](https://www.allhandsandhearts.org/blog/wellbeing-and-safety/creating-and-maintaining-a-safe-environment/); the audit does not identify the subject of its litigation, so no connection is inferred. A [board biography](https://allhandsandhearts.org/about-us/our-team) includes an oil-derivatives trading role, which merits ethical-policy inquiry but is **not evidence that the charity received fossil-fuel funding**.

- **WCK: governance and funding questions warrant a lower rank.** Its FY2025 audit, note 11, discloses **$12.286 million** in payments/expenses under an agreement with a hospital led by a board member; that disclosure establishes a related-party relationship, not wrongdoing. Its [current team page](https://wck.org/team/) lists a substantial executive/response organization alongside founder José Andrés and a CEO who is also board co-chair: institutional depth is evident, while succession resilience and independent oversight deserve confirmation. Its FY2025 audit shows no separate contract/agreement revenue, compared with $79,792 in 2024, but grants are aggregated, so that presentation alone does **not** establish zero government-derived support. The largest-donor ≤20% condition and comprehensive exclusions of arms, fossil-fuel and gambling money remain unverified.

- **Wallet verification: all five score only 2/10.** Each official route identifies a charity/processor relationship, but none of the reviewed routes supplied a permanent public donation-wallet address suitable for this report to authenticate. Thus neither dedicated-wallet control nor six-month address activity was established, and no personal/CEO wallet is endorsed. The Giving Block’s generated-address model cannot be used as evidence that a particular address has existed for six months; a long-standing crypto program is a different fact. Recurring-address reuse, custody, fees, net conversion proceeds and recipient crediting need written confirmation directly from each charity/processor before a recurring split can be considered operationally ready.

- **Effectiveness remains the largest comparative evidence gap.** The independent ratings above support accountability and financial-health assessment; they are not proof that one charity saves more lives per dollar than another. The reviewed output reports lack comparable causal outcomes and marginal-cost estimates, so E scores remain 6–7 and no common “cost per life saved” is asserted. Medical dose counts, meals, homes repaired and accounting values are different units; dividing aggregate expenses by these outputs would not create a valid cross-charity ranking.

- **Governance and speed are capabilities, not guarantees.** Annual reports, audits and public 990s are available through the linked financial sources; [Americares’ archive](https://www.americares.org/about-us/credentials/), [Direct Relief’s archive](https://www.directrelief.org/about/finance/financial-documents/) and [All Hands & Hearts’ archive](https://allhandsandhearts.org/our-financials) make the documents traceable. Long operating histories and boards support institutional continuity, but no succession plan was independently tested. Mobilizing a team, dispatching an aircraft, supporting an existing local partner and delivering aid to survivors are separate milestones; the cited 24–72-hour examples should not be represented as universal service commitments.
