# Who posts about Identity.md ($IMD) on X/Twitter?

**Research window: 7 August–7 November 2026, ending at 17:45:31 UTC on 7 November.** The start is 00:00 UTC on 7 August; these are calendar dates, not three completed calendar months.

I identified **151 accounts in 469 dated, deduplicated primary post observations**: 111 with enough content to describe an observed subject, and 40 whose evidence is mainly a brief mention, reaction, or question. **Coverage is partial. A complete archive of all posts and all accounts over the three months was not available.** The tables give the account list actually supported by the retrieved evidence.

For protocol changes, the clearest starting points in this evidence are [@surfcoderepeat](https://x.com/surfcoderepeat) and [@taylor_](https://x.com/taylor_). For explanations, see [@LastToSign](https://x.com/LastToSign), [@0xminion](https://x.com/0xminion), [@Bankless](https://x.com/Bankless), and [@Eli5defi](https://x.com/Eli5defi). For ecosystem discovery, see [@imdradar](https://x.com/imdradar) and [@theimddaily](https://x.com/theimddaily). This is a guide to subjects covered, not an endorsement or ranking of accuracy; the dated evidence is in the tables below.

**How to read the evidence.** Attribution, dates, and the linked words/posts are observations. Each “observed focus” is my interpretation of that account's IMD-related material in this sample, not a measurement of its entire timeline. “Repeated” means at least three accepted observations across at least two dates; “limited” includes a single thread or a small sample and makes the focus tentative. No minimum posting frequency was required for inclusion. Counts are lower bounds on posts observed, not total posts published. They include replies and quote posts, but exclude native reposts. Accounts can discuss several subjects; each appears once under its most useful observed category.

**Project identity.** The target is the Identity.md agent-work network described in the [project's documentation](https://imd.fun/docs/), with the Ethereum IMD token `0xd34a99bc0f67ae1bbd63c660e6d0b0dd03e263b7`. This distinguishes it from weather/business-school “IMD” results and unrelated same-symbol tokens. Ecosystem tokens such as IDM, IMDT, SHIFT, and SIMD are distinct from IMD. Discussing one is relevant here only where its connection to IMD is explicit or specifically documented as contextual evidence.

**Collection and classification method**

1. **Discovery.** I searched the public web for `$IMD`, `Identity.md`, `Identity-md`, `identitymd`, `imd.fun`, the Ethereum token contract, named contributors, and dates/months across the window. Date-restricted X-domain queries were attempted but returned little useful coverage. Additional discovery came from quoted posts/replies and the community's [closed 78-entry hackathon list](https://www.imdradar.site/data/hackathon-entries.json), including project and personal handles. Registry inclusion supplied candidates, not proof of X posting. Earlier generated research reports encountered in search were used only as leads, not as verification.
2. **Primary retrieval.** Direct public HTTPS requests could retrieve many X profile and individual-post HTML pages even when the browser/search tool's own X fetch returned 403. I decoded the embedded JSON as data without running page scripts. The saved observation binds a tweet ID to its author metadata, text/article metadata, expanded URLs, and `created_at_ms` timestamp. Dates in the main evidence are read from those timestamps, not inferred from search-crawl dates, relative “hours ago” labels, or snowflake IDs.
3. **Attribution.** Nested quoted/reposted tweets were attributed to their actual embedded author, not to the profile on which they appeared. IDs were deduplicated across profiles, threads, and individual-post pages. Native `RT @...` objects were excluded from authored counts. A quote/reply's own text was assessed separately from its quoted parent's text. A bare mention of an account by somebody else never qualified that mentioned account.
4. **Relevance.** I checked explicit project terms/contract, attached article titles, and expanded project links. Generic IMD acronym matches and mistaken handles were excluded. 11 selected observations use a specifically documented project/reply context rather than a literal project keyword; these include associated product updates and are labeled in the ledger. Those context judgments are inferences. No claim was made that every post by an ecosystem account automatically concerns IMD.
5. **Focus.** The topics are qualitative interpretations of the accepted observations. A post/thread can demonstrate a subject, but limited material cannot establish what the account “mostly” covered over all three months. The repeated/limited labels expose that distinction. Promotional copy, repeated slogans, and thread continuations can raise the observed count without adding independent corroboration.
6. **Evidence strength.** Of 469 accepted observations, 441 were recovered on their exact individual X post page (class A), and 28 were available only as fully attributed primary tweet objects embedded in another fetched X page (class B). Both are first-party payload observations; class B has a weaker retrieval trail. Secondary mirrors are labeled separately and do not inflate the primary totals. The capture manifest has 671 distinct URL records, including 201 profile URL records; repeat requests to the same URL retain one record, so this is not a count of all HTTP requests. **Uncertainty and unanswered questions**

- **Exhaustiveness remains unanswered.** Logged-out profiles generally exposed only a handful of authored posts, often including a pin; a complete paginated timeline and authenticated full-archive search were not obtained. The window was searched, but no complete three-month corpus was retrieved. Deleted/private/unindexed posts, aliases, image-only mentions, Spaces audio, and language/search variants may add accounts. Completeness and recall cannot be estimated from these sources.
- **The true dominant topic for many accounts remains unanswered.** The table establishes observed subject matter. A fuller history could change both the categories and whether a single-post account belongs on a regular follow list. Handles and display names can also change; this is a retrieval-time account map.
- **Affiliation and incentives remain partly unknown.** Self-description and participation do not prove employment, official status, account ownership, independence, undisclosed holdings, or compensation for promotion. I did not infer wallet ownership from display names or holder lists.
- **Underlying product claims remain unverified here.** I did not test every app, audit contracts, reconstruct payouts, verify every reported network statistic, or check the competition leaderboard independently. Historical risk statements may have become stale; they are evidence of what an author discussed at the time, not a current security assessment.
- **Open support outcomes remain unanswered.** Complaints and project responses are both linked, but resolution is not established without transaction-level evidence from the affected case.

A complete answer to the literal “all posts / all accounts” request would require an authorized, paginated archive covering this date window, with replies, quote posts, repost events, and handle changes, followed by the same project disambiguation and author/date checks. This report delivers the useful list that could actually be supported, and records that remaining coverage gap.

**Local checks and provenance limits**

The accompanying offline checker verifies table/ledger consistency, unique IDs and handles, author-to-URL binding, time-window inclusion, short-excerpt limits, representative-post links, and reported counts. Those checks do not certify the truth of a post or independently establish completeness. SHA-256 values identify the fetched pages; full HTML captures are not part of the deliverable, and a hash alone is not independent verification. The report and compact evidence can be read and checked without network access.
