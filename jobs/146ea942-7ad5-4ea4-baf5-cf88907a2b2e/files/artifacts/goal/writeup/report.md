## Why the score target is conditional

The assignment gives the base's Paradigm mean as **106.804593**, and the four spread-specific gains as **+0.775951**, **+0.065331**, **+0.164311**, and **+0.574129**. The corresponding policies are: a larger front tick-2 ask below one cent with front-ask retention; own-flow-scaled one-cent stock; the **20 dollars** ahead rule for a low front ask at the rival tick; and own-fill retail-flow estimation for ask and stock sizing. Because each run has one fixed spread, exact per-run preservation would make the assembled score **108.384315** on those same **200** Paradigm simulations. This is the assignment's arithmetic target, not a fresh score measured by these items. The goal describes progress from **107.58** to **108.38**, with first place **109.93**. [G]

All five items report `base_mean_edge` **108.63974289615767**, but their calibration notes identify that as the **parent** result, not the base's Paradigm score. Their actual unseen-base means are **92.95847243878374** for A/C/F and **92.612021147951** for B/D. These are different local samples; none substitutes for **106.804593** or validates the projected leaderboard score. [A–F, G]

The assignment also reports Demand **+0.62** and Cash **+0.43** on Paradigm, and Demand with floors **+0.55** on unseen runs, not entered. Those changes were built on the parent and acted across all spreads. These figures do not establish the gain from using them only in spread 2, nor can they simply be added to the exact-stack target. [G]

## How much weight to put on the alternatives

C and F compare against the original flow-stock policy on the same **1987** spread-2 runs selected from `[60000,68000)`. That original policy gains **0.6075141093718673**, SE **0.18318063780805277**, over the base. Cash's paired advantage over flow is **0.1795134131880722**, SE **0.401422244113473**: its own result explicitly says the advantage is not significant. F retains floors because its mean exceeds the original policy's mean, but supplies no standard error for the paired **floors-minus-flow** difference. Its **0.15359022930502778** is the SE against the base, not the SE of improvement over flow. [C, F]

D uses only the first **993** eligible spread-2 seeds in the same interval. Its original-policy baseline gain is **0.41189538722034263**, SE **0.19859171263231082**, versus Demand's **0.9757539952248745**, SE **0.48213013327879434**. D provides no paired Demand-minus-flow standard error. Its larger candidate mean cannot establish superiority to Cash or floors measured on a different sample. [D]

The untouched all-spread holdout also gives no decisive replacement ranking: F's mean exceeds A's on their shared holdout, while C's is lower than A's; D's is lower than B's on their shared shorter holdout. No item supplies the paired replacement-minus-exact-stack SE on that holdout. The recommendation to try floors is therefore an experimental priority, not a claimed proven improvement on Paradigm. Cash remains plausible but noisy; Demand has a smaller comparison sample and no demonstrated all-spread holdout advantage over B. [A–F; interpretation of their reported means and methods]

## Exactness and source limitations

A reports two exactness sets, starting at seeds **60000** and **64000**, each taking the first **300** matching seeds per spread and comparing every result field. B reports only the first **150** per spread from **60000** and explicitly omits the second set from **64000**. D reports an initial pure-stack check and final replacement check at **150** per spread; F reports both at **300** per spread; C reports an initial pure-stack check at **300** per spread and final Cash equality as described in the table. These are item-authored replay claims; this write-up did not rerun strategies or inspect their raw replay logs. Preserving numerical operation order matters: C specifically warns that Python's `sum()` and explicit loops can round differently, while B says it copied the spread-2 handler verbatim. [A–F]

The assignment flags **4 sources for stack-a**, **1 source for stack-b**, and **3 sources for stack-cash2** because rereads failed or did not support the quoted claims. The supplied result JSONs contain no source lists, failed quotations, or mapping from each flag to a particular assertion. Thus the affected claims cannot be identified more narrowly from these inputs. Treat A/B/C's source-backed assertions about faithful grafts and reproduction as qualified, rather than dismissing their numerical reports or treating them as independently verified. A's wider replay claim does not erase its larger source-verification gap; C's promising gain does not erase its flags. B's own flag also remains unresolved. [G; inspection of A, B, C]

D and F have no source flags named in the assignment; that is not independent validation. F's claim that all source quotes were fetched and checked is itself an item claim. The public simulator repository was readable during this write-up and describes a passive strategy in a binary FIFO order book, but the base artifact URL was inaccessible through the web reader. That attempt neither verifies the artifact nor proves it unavailable by every method. No source-level or runtime reproduction is claimed here. [F; public repository; retrieval observation]
